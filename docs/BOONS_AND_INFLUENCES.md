# Boons and Influences

This document defines Boons, Influences, their sources, their costs, and their role within Swamp Magic.

Boons are not traditional rewards.

Boons are opportunities.

Every Boon comes from somewhere.

Every Boon has a cost.

The player may not immediately understand that cost.

---

# Core Philosophy

Boons offer power, convenience, access, knowledge, or opportunity.

Boons should appear attractive.

Boons should solve problems.

Boons should create new problems.

The player should frequently believe:

"I got away with something."

Only later realizing:

"Something got hold of me."

---

# What Is A Boon?

A Boon is a lasting advantage granted by a person, faction, belief system, ritual, organization, or unknown influence.

A Boon may provide:

- New opportunities
- New abilities
- Special knowledge
- Unique interactions
- Increased effectiveness
- Alternative solutions

Boons are not free upgrades.

Boons create Influence.

---

# What Is Influence?

Influence represents a lasting connection between the player and the source of a Boon.

Influence may represent:

- Loyalty
- Obligation
- Debt
- Faith
- Corruption
- Exposure
- Affiliation
- Dependence

Influence exists whether the player acknowledges it or not.

Influence is tracked by the game.

Influence is not normally shown to the player.

---

# Boons And Rewards

Boons are not ordinary rewards.

Ordinary rewards are earned.

Boons are offered.

A reward is payment.

A Boon is an opportunity.

The difference matters.

---

# Sources Of Boons

Possible sources include:

- Individuals
- Families
- Communities
- Factions
- Religious groups
- Cults
- Rituals
- The Swamp
- Unknown influences

Different sources may grant similar Boons for different reasons.

The source is often more important than the Boon itself.

---

# Visible Benefits

The player should generally understand what a Boon appears to provide.

Examples:

- Improved night vision
- Better survival ability
- Faster recovery
- Easier social access
- Special dialogue options
- Recognition from a group

Players should have a reason to accept a Boon.

---

# Hidden Costs

The cost of a Boon should rarely be fully visible.

Possible costs include:

- Relationship changes
- Closed opportunities
- New enemies
- New obligations
- Influence growth
- Reputation shifts
- Future restrictions
- Altered perceptions

The player should not receive a complete list of consequences.

Boons should create uncertainty.

---

# Learning The Cost

While costs are not automatically revealed, players may discover them through:

- Awareness
- Knowledge
- Investigation
- Experience
- Strong relationships
- Observant NPCs
- Warnings from trusted people

The world contains people who understand Boons better than the player.

Listening to them may be valuable.

---

# Influence Is Not Always Hostile

Not every Influence represents corruption.

Some Influences may be:

- Protective
- Beneficial
- Cooperative
- Sympathetic

Even beneficial Influences create obligations.

An Influence is a relationship.

Not a morality score.

---

# Boons And Relationships

Taking a Boon may affect relationships.

Possible outcomes include:

- New allies
- Suspicious allies
- New enemies
- Lost opportunities
- Unexpected trust
- Unexpected distrust

The source of a Boon matters.

People notice affiliations.

People notice changes.

People notice favors.

---

# Boons And Reputation

Reputation may be affected by:

- Accepting a Boon
- Rejecting a Boon
- Revealing a Boon
- Concealing a Boon

Some groups may view a Boon positively.

Others may view the same Boon negatively.

No Boon exists in isolation.

---

# Boons And Cases

Boons may:

- Reveal opportunities
- Create shortcuts
- Open alternate solutions
- Alter investigation approaches

Boons may never:

- Resolve a Protected Mystery
- Reveal objective truth
- Bypass investigation entirely

Boons change the path.

They do not eliminate the journey.

---

# Temptation

Many Boons exist because they are tempting.

The easiest solution is often the most dangerous.

Players should regularly encounter choices where:

- Effort is difficult.
- Patience is slow.
- The shortcut is attractive.

The player must decide what the shortcut is worth.

---

# Influence Growth

Influence may increase through:

- Accepting Boons
- Repeated cooperation
- Participation in rituals
- Shared beliefs
- Ongoing obligations
- Certain major decisions

Influence should grow naturally from behavior.

---

# Permanent Consequences

Some Influences may create permanent consequences.

Examples may include:

- Changed relationships
- Lost opportunities
- New dialogues
- New restrictions
- New obligations

Permanent consequences should create new gameplay rather than remove gameplay.

The goal is adaptation.

Not punishment.

---

# Removal

Some Influences may be removed.

Removal should never be trivial.

Removal may require:

- Sacrifice
- Effort
- Relationships
- Favors
- Rare opportunities

Removing an Influence may create new consequences.

The player should carefully consider whether removal is worth the cost.

---

# Hidden Paths

Certain conversations, opportunities, investigations, and endings may become available only because of specific Influences.

Likewise, some paths may become unavailable.

Influences help shape a unique playthrough.

No playthrough should reveal everything.

---

# The World Remembers

People notice associations.

People form opinions.

Communities remember decisions.

Influence changes how the world sees the player.

The player may not always realize these changes are happening.

---

# Core Principle

Every Boon comes from somewhere.

Every Boon changes something.

Every Boon costs something.

The player is not always expected to understand the price when it is offered.

The world understands.

Eventually.
