# Character Progression

This document defines how player characters grow and change throughout Swamp Magic.

Character Progression is not intended to function as a traditional experience-point or level-based system.

Progression reflects what a character learns, who they become, and how Ferry Landing changes them.

---

# Core Philosophy

A character grows through participation in the world.

Growth comes from:

- Investigation
- Relationships
- Trust
- Respect
- Hardship
- Discovery
- Mistakes
- Consequences

The goal is not to accumulate levels.

The goal is to become someone different from the person who arrived in Ferry Landing.

---

# Sources of Growth

Growth may be earned through many forms of interaction.

Examples include:

- Solving Cases
- Advancing Cases
- Building relationships
- Repairing relationships
- Completing Briefs
- Helping communities
- Learning new information
- Discovering contradictions
- Enduring hardship
- Surviving dangerous situations
- Exploring difficult questions

There is no single activity that exclusively drives growth.

---

# Cases and Growth

Cases are one of the primary sources of growth.

A Case represents a meaningful investigation involving Questions, Evidence, Contradictions, and Beliefs.

Completing a Case should provide growth opportunities.

However, completing a Case is not the only source of growth.

---

# Case Resolution

A Case may be settled whenever the player believes they have reached a sufficient understanding.

The game does not require a Case to be fully explored before settlement.

Players may settle Cases:

- Quickly
- Carefully
- Cautiously
- Recklessly

Each approach has consequences.

---

# Early Settlement

A player may choose to settle a Case with limited information.

Benefits:

- Faster resolution
- Immediate rewards
- Earlier access to other opportunities

Costs:

- Missed evidence
- Missed relationships
- Missed growth opportunities
- Incorrect conclusions
- Reduced understanding

A Case may be settled before all information is discovered.

---

# Understanding vs Certainty

Players are not rewarded for finding the "correct answer."

Players are rewarded for engagement with the investigation.

A character who rushes to conclusions may finish more Cases.

A character who investigates thoroughly may gain:

- Additional growth
- Additional relationships
- Additional opportunities
- Additional understanding

Neither approach guarantees certainty.

---

# Settled Cases

When a Case is settled, the world responds.

People react to the player's conclusion.

Relationships may change.

Opportunities may open.

Opportunities may close.

Trust may increase or decrease.

Settling a Case changes the world.

---

# Revisiting Cases

A settled Case may later encounter:

- New evidence
- New witnesses
- New contradictions
- New information

This does not automatically reopen the Case.

The player must decide how to respond.

---

# Challenging Previous Conclusions

When new evidence contradicts a settled Case, the player may:

- Ignore it
- Conceal it
- Investigate it
- Publicly challenge their previous conclusion

Each choice has consequences.

The world remembers what the player previously believed.

---

# Reopening a Case

Reopening a Case is a significant action.

It is not intended to be a free retry mechanic.

Potential consequences include:

- Loss of Trust
- Loss of Respect
- Damaged Relationships
- Reduced Standing
- New Opportunities
- New Problems

Communities develop opinions.

Changing a public position can strengthen or weaken those opinions.

---

# Reputation and Respect

Respect is a form of social capital.

Respect is earned through:

- Competence
- Reliability
- Service
- Integrity
- Consistency

Respect is not currency.

Respect is not a score.

Respect represents how others view the player.

Respect influences:

- Opportunities
- Relationships
- Access
- Recommendations
- Cooperation

The player spends Respect whenever they ask others to trust their judgment.

---

# Relationships

Relationships are progression.

Relationships are not rewards.

Strong relationships may provide:

- Information
- Favors
- Opportunities
- Protection
- Access

Damaged relationships can be repaired.

Repairing relationships should require effort.

Trust takes time to build and time to restore.

---

# Attributes

Attributes represent who the character is becoming.

Attributes grow slowly.

Attribute growth should reflect meaningful personal change.

Examples:

- Enduring hardship may improve Fortitude.
- Building influence may improve Presence.
- Study and investigation may improve Knowledge.
- Persistence through adversity may improve Will.

Attributes should not increase simply because time passed.

Growth should be earned through experience.

---

# Skills

Skills represent learned capabilities.

Skills improve more often than Attributes.

Skill growth reflects practice and experience.

Examples:

- Frequent investigation improves investigative expertise.
- Community involvement improves social capabilities.
- Research improves intellectual capabilities.
- Survival improves through hardship and travel.

Skills reflect what the player has learned to do.

---

# Conditions

Conditions represent what is happening to the character.

Examples include:

- Awareness
- Injury
- Fatigue
- Standing
- Reputation

Conditions change more frequently than Skills or Attributes.

Conditions are not progression rewards.

Conditions are consequences.

---

# Boons

Boons are opportunities for accelerated growth or unusual advantages.

Boons may provide:

- New abilities
- New opportunities
- Unusual insights
- Unique advantages

Boons should appear beneficial.

Boons are rarely free.

---

# Hidden Costs

Every Boon comes from a source.

Examples may include:

- Individuals
- Communities
- Factions
- Belief systems
- Rituals
- Unexplained influences

Accepting a Boon may create consequences that are not immediately visible.

These consequences may affect:

- Relationships
- Standing
- Access
- Opportunities
- Future choices

The player is not always expected to understand the cost when accepting a Boon.

---

# Permanent Consequences

Some choices permanently influence the character.

Examples may include:

- Alliances
- Boons
- Public positions
- Significant decisions

Permanent consequences should create new challenges rather than end the game.

The player should adapt to their choices rather than erase them.

---

# Growth and Temptation

Characters may pursue growth through effort.

Characters may pursue growth through shortcuts.

Shortcuts are often tempting.

Shortcuts are often costly.

The player is not required to accept a shortcut.

The player is not guaranteed to understand the consequences if they do.

---

# Core Principle

Progression is not about becoming stronger.

Progression is about becoming someone.

The choices a player makes determine who that person becomes.

Ferry Landing changes everyone who stays.

The player decides how.
