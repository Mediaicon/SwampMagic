# Personal Mysteries

## Purpose

Personal Mysteries are player-origin investigations.

Each Personal Mystery begins with an Origin selected during character creation. It gives the player a personal reason to investigate, a starting perspective, and an unresolved question that may develop alongside broader Cases and Questions.

Personal Mysteries are **not** Protected Mysteries.

They are intended to reach meaningful conclusions. Those conclusions may generate additional questions, competing interpretations, or new beliefs.

A Personal Mystery may connect to a Protected Mystery, but may never resolve it or establish objective truth about it.

The investigation model remains:

**Evidence → Interpretation → Belief**

A meaningful conclusion does not require the game to confirm that the player's belief is objectively correct.

---

## Current Implementation Status

The Origins and Personal Mystery premises documented below reflect the Origins currently implemented in the project.

Origin names, contacts, and Personal Mystery premises are currently provisional content. This document records their present narrative boundaries and should be updated when their authored content changes.

No new Origins are defined here.

# Investigator

## Personal Mystery

### The Missing Client

A client hired the player to find someone last seen heading for Ferry Landing.

The disappearance is personal to the Investigator because it represents unfinished work and an unresolved responsibility.

## Themes

- Responsibility
- Trust
- Obsession
- Interpretation
- The limits of professional certainty

## Status

**Implemented Origin; Personal Mystery premise is provisional.**

The Personal Mystery is intended to reach a meaningful conclusion.

That conclusion may not establish a single objective account of what happened to the missing person.

## Possible Connections

- Ferry Landing
- The Original Preacher
- The Church Fire
- Other missing or disputed accounts

Connections to larger mysteries should provide context, evidence, or competing interpretations rather than convert those mysteries into solvable answers.

## Protected Mystery Boundaries

The Missing Client may encounter information related to **PM-1: Who burned the church?**

It may not:

- Identify who burned the church as objective fact.
- Confirm that the missing client was responsible for the fire.
- Eliminate competing beliefs about the fire.
- Resolve PM-1 through the Personal Mystery's conclusion.

The Investigator's mystery must remain a mystery about the missing client, even when larger uncertainties affect its interpretation.

# Federal Agent

## Personal Mystery

### The Misdirected Payments

A routine audit traced payments to Ferry Landing. The player's superiors told them to close the file.

The mystery concerns both the payments themselves and the decision to stop investigating them.

## Themes

- Institutional authority
- Missing information
- Obedience
- Suspicion
- The difference between a closed file and a resolved question

## Status

**Implemented Origin; Personal Mystery premise is provisional.**

The Personal Mystery is intended to reach a meaningful conclusion.

A conclusion may explain part of the player's personal question without establishing that every irregularity has a single hidden cause.

## Possible Connections

- Ferry Landing
- Institutional records
- Missing or incomplete documentation
- The Church Fire, where independently relevant evidence creates a connection

The existence of an institutional connection does not establish that an institution possesses objective knowledge of the larger mysteries surrounding Ferry Landing.

## Protected Mystery Boundaries

The Misdirected Payments may connect to a Protected Mystery through records, testimony, or competing interpretations.

It may not:

- Use financial or official records to establish objective truth about a Protected Mystery.
- Treat a government or institutional account as final authority over PM-1.
- Convert an unexplained payment into proof of who burned the church.

The resolution of the Personal Mystery must remain distinct from the resolution of any Protected Mystery.

# Deputy

## Personal Mystery

### The Unclaimed Report

A missing-persons report sat in the player's desk for three years with a Ferry Landing postmark.

Nobody wanted it.

The mystery begins with the player's relationship to an investigation that was neglected, avoided, or allowed to remain unresolved.

## Themes

- Responsibility
- Neglect
- Memory
- Institutional silence
- The consequences of choosing not to know

## Status

**Implemented Origin; Personal Mystery premise is provisional.**

The Personal Mystery is intended to reach a meaningful conclusion.

That conclusion may leave uncertainty about the missing person, the reasons the report was ignored, or what other people believed at the time.

## Possible Connections

- Missing-person investigations
- Ferry Landing
- Local testimony
- The New Preacher
- Older or incomplete records

These connections should create additional investigative questions rather than provide an authoritative explanation for Ferry Landing or its history.

## Protected Mystery Boundaries

The Unclaimed Report may contain information that touches on Protected Mysteries.

It may not:

- Establish objective truth about PM-1 or any other Protected Mystery.
- Turn an old report into definitive historical proof.
- Require the player to accept one interpretation of disputed events as fact.

An official document remains evidence to be interpreted, not automatic proof.

# Occult Researcher

## Personal Mystery

### The Crossed-Out Date

The player's notes point to Ferry Landing, to a name and a date that someone crossed out.

The mystery begins with an alteration to the historical or recorded account and the question of what that alteration means.

## Themes

- Knowledge
- Obsession
- Erasure
- Interpretation
- The danger of believing understanding will make uncertainty safe

## Status

**Implemented Origin; Personal Mystery premise is provisional.**

The Personal Mystery is intended to reach a meaningful conclusion.

Discovering why a detail was crossed out does not require the game to confirm that the restored detail represents objective truth.

## Possible Connections

- Ferry Landing
- Historical records
- Names and dates
- Iron and Fire
- Conflicting interpretations of older events

Research and recognition may reveal additional evidence, but evidence remains open to interpretation.

## Protected Mystery Boundaries

The Crossed-Out Date has a heightened risk of drifting into Protected Mystery territory because erased information can be mistaken for a hidden answer.

The Personal Mystery may:

- Reveal that information was altered or omitted.
- Provide evidence relevant to competing interpretations.
- Raise new questions about why a record was changed.

It may not:

- Restore a missing detail and present it as the objective answer to a Protected Mystery.
- Use historical research to definitively answer PM-1.
- establish that greater knowledge removes uncertainty.

The discovery of previously hidden information should often complicate understanding rather than resolve it.

# Governance Rules for Personal Mysteries

All Personal Mysteries should follow these boundaries:

1. **Personal Mysteries begin with the player.**  
   They should provide a personal reason to investigate without determining the player's eventual beliefs.

2. **Personal Mysteries may reach meaningful conclusions.**  
   A meaningful conclusion does not require a single objectively confirmed answer.

3. **Personal Mysteries may connect to Protected Mysteries.**  
   They may contribute evidence, interpretations, contradictions, and beliefs.

4. **Personal Mysteries may never resolve Protected Mysteries.**  
   No Personal Mystery conclusion may establish objective truth where a Protected Mystery preserves uncertainty.

5. **Evidence is not proof.**  
   Documents, testimony, records, memories, and discoveries remain evidence to be interpreted.

6. **Contradictions are content.**  
   A contradiction within a Personal Mystery should create an investigation opportunity rather than automatically identify a lie or error.

7. **Origin provides perspective, not destiny.**  
   Origins are backgrounds, not classes. An Origin should shape the player's starting perspective and Personal Mystery without locking the player into a predetermined conclusion.
